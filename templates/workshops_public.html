Les Ateliers

Alongside the restoration of the building, we have been recovering something less visible — the crafts, traditions and rituals that once gave a house of this size its life.

The ateliers are where that work and our guests meet. Each takes its subject from a different corner of the château: the kitchen and its copper, the linen room, the gardens, the hunt for what has been lost and must be found again. Artisans come from the valley and from further afield, and they teach in the rooms themselves rather than in a classroom.

Numbers are small by design — rarely more than a dozen. There is no obligation to fill your days. Join what interests you, or take a book to a quiet salon and let the hours pass.

The Cooking

More of an atelier happens around the table than anywhere else.

Our chef works from the Sunday morning market in the village square, from producers she has known for years, and in summer from the château's own gardens — herbs, cherries, plums, raspberries. Bread and pastries come from the boulangerie in Les Cabannes, three minutes' walk below the gates.

The kitchen itself was one of the first rooms recovered, and cooking ateliers are taught in it. Dietary requirements are designed into the menus rather than worked around on the night — send them when you register and they become part of the planning.

A Day At The Château

  • MorningBreakfast in the dining room from eight — viennoiseries carried up from the boulangerie, preserves, and teas made from herbs grown here. The first session begins when everybody has finished, and not before.
  • AfternoonWorking sessions, or the gardens, the pool, the tennis court, and the valley. Some guests walk to the village. Some do not leave their chair.
  • EveningAn aperitif in the Salon de Rose, then dinner at the long table. Courses drawn from the market that week, and conversation that tends to outlast them.

What an atelier is, and is not

What you will find

  • Small groups, rarely more than a dozen
  • Artisans teaching in the rooms where the work is done
  • Every meal, every session, every material included
  • Time that is genuinely yours to use or not

What you will not

  • A hotel programme with a timetable to keep
  • A finished château — you will be working amongst the restoration
  • Ensuite bathrooms in most rooms; the law protecting the house forbids them
  • Anything you must have done before

The crafts return with the building

What is taught here was very nearly lost, and so was the room it is taught in. Both are being brought back to the same standard, and by the same patience.
