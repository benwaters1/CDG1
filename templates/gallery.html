The château, as it is

Photographs taken across the seasons, mostly by guests and by the family, over a decade of work.

There is no styling here and very little tidying. Rooms are photographed as they are on the day — some finished, some with the plaster still open and the scaffolding up. That is the point of the record.

Bare plaster and finished salons, the grounds in every season, and the work itself. No staging, and nothing hidden.

Stay while the work continues

Every photograph here is a room that was, at some point, unusable. Four of the ninety-four were habitable in 2013; the rest had caved-in roofs and trees growing through them.

The work continues because people come. A night, a table, a week at an atelier — and then the next room, the next window, the next stretch of fresco lifted out from under a century of overpaint.

Five bedrooms, open to guests. Breakfast, a private tour and a guided walk of the gardens and village are included with every one.

Bare plaster and weathered stone, protected by law. Inside that, antique French beds, vintage monogrammed linen, hand-relaid floors, and bathrooms finished to a standard the rest of the château is still years from. The imperfection is deliberate, and it is what these photographs are of.

The Long View

Eight Hundred Years

What you are looking at is the fourth or fifth thing to stand on this rock.

1213

A fortification, documented

The first written record of a structure here, counted among the fortifications of the county. Château-Verdun takes its name from the medieval fort.

1580

The castle destroyed

The fortress that stood here before this one was pulled down. The de Sales family held the land for centuries either side of it.

1741

Ten years of building

Louis Gaspard de Sales, Marquis de Gudanes and known as the King of the Pyrénées, commissioned Ange-Jacques Gabriel. Construction ran to 1750.

1750s

The place to be wined and dined

Aristocrats, artists and writers came for it. Voltaire and Diderot among them.

1850

Empty

The last year anyone lived here. A century and a half of weather, and a spell as a school holiday camp in the 1960s.

1994

Classified

Class I Historic Monument, 14 June. By then trees were growing through the roof.

2013

Bought

Four rooms of ninety-four could be entered. Everything photographed here has happened since.

Fifteen Years of It

Rooms That No Longer Look Like This

Photographs taken since 2013, of rooms in states that have gone and will not come back. A wall opened, a floor lifted, a ceiling before it was consolidated. Once a room is finished, that version of it exists only here.

It is the reason the record was kept at all. It is also, unexpectedly, the most useful thing on this site for anyone scouting a location — the same room can be seen stripped to stone and finished, which is a choice no other building can offer.
