The château, as it is

Photographs taken across the seasons, mostly by guests and by the family, over a decade of work.

There is no styling here and very little tidying. Rooms are photographed as they are on the day — some finished, some with the plaster still open and the scaffolding up. That is the point of the record.

Bare plaster and finished salons, the grounds in every season, and the work itself. No staging, and nothing hidden.

Stay while the work continues

Every photograph here is a room that was, at some point, unusable. Four of the ninety-four were habitable in 2013; the rest had caved-in roofs and trees growing through them.

The work continues because people come. A night booked, a table taken, a week on a workshop — each pays for the next room, the next window, the next stretch of fresco lifted out from under a century of overpaint.

Seven bedrooms, open to guests. Breakfast, a private tour and a guided walk of the gardens and village are included with every one.

Check availability

Gudanes is a Class I Historic Monument, which means the walls stay as the centuries left them — bare plaster, weathered stone, protected by law and not ours to change. Everything within them is new: antique French beds dressed in vintage monogrammed linen, floors relaid by hand, bathrooms finished to a standard the rest of the château has not reached yet. The imperfection is deliberate, and staying here pays for the next room.
