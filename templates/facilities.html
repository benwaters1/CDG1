Skiing at Ax 3 Domaines

Twenty-five minutes from the gates. Eighty kilometres of piste between 1,400 and 2,400 metres, twenty-one lifts, and three linked domains — Bonascre, Saquet and Les Campels. Green through black, a snowpark, a boardercross, and two dedicated beginner areas.

A gondola runs from the centre of Ax-les-Thermes to the slopes in under ten minutes, so there is no mountain road to drive in the morning. The climb has finished a Tour de France stage five times.

We arrange the whole of it. Lift passes bought and in your hand before you leave. Transport there and back each day. Hire fitted at the resort — skis, boards, boots, poles, helmets — and an ESF or ESI instructor for any age and any standard.

Tell us when you book and it is simply organised. You come down to breakfast and the day is already arranged.

L'Hiver

Winter in the Valley

Snow to the gates, fires lit in the salons, and a lift twenty-five minutes up the road.

The château does not close for winter. It is arguably the better season — the valley empties, the mountain fills, and the house is at its warmest when it is cold outside. Skis, boots and helmets are arranged before you arrive, and an ESF or ESI instructor for any age and any standard.

Outside

The Gardens Are Terraced

The ground falls away from the house in stages. Formal terraces first, then steps, then le parc — the wooded parkland that runs to the boundary.

Old photographs show those terraces fenced with intricate ironwork. A few iron posts are still standing, and that is all that survives of it. The rest is known only from the pictures.

The limestone walls around the estate are original. They were here before the house was.

Three Minutes From the Gates

You Can Come By Train

Les Cabannes is on the Toulouse–Latour-de-Carol line, three minutes below the château. Most people assume a valley in the Pyrénées means hiring a car. It does not.

  1. From Paris Gare de Lyon or Montparnasse to Toulouse by TGV, then the regional line south. A long day, and all of it seated.
  2. From Toulouse Matabiau to Les Cabannes, direct. The last hour runs up the Ariège with the mountains closing in on both sides.
  3. From Barcelona Over the border to Latour-de-Carol, then north down the same line. Slower than driving and considerably better.
  4. Last three minutes Tell us your train and someone will be at the station. There is no taxi rank.

Once you are here, the village, the market and the restaurants are walkable. For Beille, Ax-les-Thermes or the caves you will want wheels — we can arrange a car for a day rather than a week.

Worth knowingThe line is scenic rather than fast, and the last connection of the day is early. Send us your booking and we will tell you if it works.

Beyond the Gates

What the Valley Gives You

The question most people ask is what there is to do for four days. The answer is that the château is the quiet part.

Sunday, Les Cabannes

The market is three minutes down the hill. The chef has bought from the same producers for twenty years and will tell you which stall is worth queueing at. What comes back is what you eat.

The mountain above

Beille is a twenty-minute drive: cross-country skiing and sledging in winter, walking and mountain biking once the snow goes. The Pyrenean peaks start at the end of the drive.

Thermal water

Ax-les-Thermes has been a spa town since the thirteenth century. The hot springs still run through the middle of it, and the basins in the square are free.

Older than the house

The painted caves of the Ariège are among the oldest in Europe. Niaux is half an hour away and its bison were drawn roughly thirteen thousand years before Gabriel drew the façade.

Getting aboutA car is worth having. The train reaches Les Cabannes, but the valley rewards wandering.
