La Table

Dinner at the château, cooked by our own chefs and served in the dining room by candlelight.

Meals are arranged in advance rather than served to order, so that the kitchen buys for the table it is cooking for. Lunch is available on the same terms.

There is no fixed menu. What you eat depends on the Sunday market in the village, on the producers the chef buys from, and in summer on the château gardens.

Where It Comes From

Most of it comes from within a few miles.

Bread and pastries come from the boulangerie in Les Cabannes, three minutes' walk below the gates, and arrive while they are still warm. The Sunday morning market in the village square supplies most of the rest — vegetables, cheeses from the mountain farms, and charcuterie.

The château gardens give herbs through the year, and in summer cherries, plums and raspberries. Trout comes from the Ariège. Lamb comes from the Pyrénées, sometimes from the valley visible out of the dining room windows.

La Salle à Manger

The room in which dinner is served was among the most damaged in the château.

Its ceiling had gone. What remained of the panelling was under later paint, and the floor beneath it had rotted through in places. It took several years to bring back — the plaster consolidated rather than replaced, the panelling cleaned by hand, and a new floor laid in oak beneath walls left deliberately as they were found.

Dinner is served by candlelight in the dining room. In winter the fire is lit.

Through The Day

  • Petit-déjeunerEight until ten, included with every stay. Viennoiseries from the village, local yoghurts and preserves, and teas made from herbs grown in the gardens.
  • Salon de ThéOpen from morning until evening. French and local teas, coffee, and hot water refreshed through the day.
  • DéjeunerBy arrangement, and often taken outside when the weather allows — beneath the façade, or in the gardens above the valley.
  • DînerServed from half past seven, by candlelight in the dining room.

Occasions

Birthdays, anniversaries and proposals are arranged with the chef — styling, flowers, menu cards, and a table set apart from the rest if you would prefer it. Tell us what the evening is for when you reserve.

The kitchen was the first room brought back

It was recovered before the bedrooms, before the salons, and before anything else that guests now see. A house of this kind is organised around its table, and always has been.

Gudanes is a Class I Historic Monument, which means the walls stay as the centuries left them — bare plaster, weathered stone, protected by law and not ours to change. Everything within them is new: antique French beds dressed in vintage monogrammed linen, floors relaid by hand, bathrooms finished to a standard the rest of the château has not reached yet. The imperfection is deliberate, and staying here pays for the next room.
