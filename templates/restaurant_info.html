La Table

There is no restaurant at Château de Gudanes, and there was never meant to be.

There is a chef, a dining room recovered over several years, and a table that seats the house. Lunches and dinners are arranged in advance and cooked for whoever is staying — most evenings a single sitting, occasionally two, and never so many that the kitchen loses sight of the room.

Menus are built around the week rather than a card. What arrives depends on the Sunday market in the village square, on producers our chef has known for years, and in summer on the château's own gardens.

Where It Comes From

Almost nothing travels far to reach this table.

Bread and pastries come from the boulangerie in Les Cabannes, three minutes' walk below the gates, and arrive while they are still warm. The Sunday morning market in the village square supplies most of the rest — vegetables, cheeses from the mountain farms, charcuterie, and whatever the season has decided upon.

The château gardens give herbs through the year, and in summer cherries, plums and raspberries. Trout comes from the Ariège. Lamb comes from the Pyrénées, sometimes from the valley visible out of the dining room windows.

The Dining Room

The room in which dinner is served was among the most damaged in the house.

Its ceiling had gone. What remained of the panelling was under later paint, and the floor beneath it had rotted through in places. It took several years to bring back — the plaster consolidated rather than replaced, the panelling cleaned by hand, and a new floor laid in oak beneath walls left deliberately as they were found.

Dinner is served by candlelight at a long table, and the room is at its best in winter, when the fire is lit and the valley outside has gone entirely quiet.

The Menus

Two courses, three courses, or a full degustation. Wine may be paired throughout, drawn largely from the south-west.

Dietary requirements and allergies are designed into the menu rather than worked around on the night. Tell us when you reserve and they become part of the planning — not an adjustment made in the kitchen at eight o'clock.

Through The Day

  • Petit-déjeunerEight until ten, included with every stay. Viennoiseries from the village, local yoghurts and preserves, and teas made from herbs grown in the gardens.
  • Salon de ThéOpen from morning until evening. French and local teas, coffee, and hot water refreshed through the day.
  • DéjeunerBy arrangement, and often taken outside when the weather allows — beneath the façade, or in the gardens above the valley.
  • DînerServed from half past seven. A single sitting, at a long table, by candlelight.

Occasions

Birthdays, anniversaries and proposals are arranged with the chef — styling, flowers, menu cards, and a table set apart from the rest if you would prefer it. Tell us what the evening is for when you reserve.

The kitchen was the first room brought back

It was recovered before the bedrooms, before the salons, and before anything else that guests now see. A house of this kind is organised around its table, and always has been.
