Restaurer, pas rénover Restore, not renovate

The Principle

Why the Walls Stay

A renovation makes a house new. A restoration lets it return. At Gudanes the law and the family want the same thing: the walls are kept as the centuries left them, the plaster and stone read and recorded rather than replaced, and everything new is placed within them, never over them.

So the bare walls are not unfinished work, and they are not an apology. They are the house itself, and the reason it is worth coming to.

Monument historique A Historic Monument

French Heritage Law

What a Historic Monument Is

In France, the buildings that matter most to the nation's history and art are protected by law as monuments historiques. The protection goes back to a law of 1913, and it comes at two levels.

Classé · the highest level

For buildings whose preservation is in the public interest, for their history or their art. Only around 15,000 buildings in France are classé. Any work on them needs the state's approval, and is carried out under its scrutiny.

Inscrit · the second level

For buildings of enough interest to be worth preserving. Around 31,000 buildings are inscrit. Work on them must be declared, and the rules are lighter.

Château de Gudanes is classé, and has been a Historic Monument since 1994. It is why every intervention is applied for and approved before work begins, why original stone and timber are kept rather than replaced, and why the walls stay as they are. It is also why a night here is an experience unlike a night anywhere else.

À l’intérieur Inside

Inside

What You Will See

Rooms in every state: some brought back and lived in, some opened up and left as they were found, some still waiting behind closed doors. Plaster as the centuries left it, painted surfaces coming back to the light, and the work itself, slow and deliberate.

A night here is your room, breakfast and the lounge. The rest of the house, the rooms brought back and the rooms still waiting, is opened to the workshops.

A Restoration, Not a Renovation

Restoration answers for every decision: each intervention approved by the Monuments Historiques before a tool is lifted, overpaint lifted by hand, original stone and timber conserved rather than replaced. It is the slower and more exacting course, and the only one worth taking with a house of this consequence.

Staying Here

Guests Are Part of It

The house is open while it is still coming back, which is an unusual thing to be able to say: bare walls on one side of the door, new floors and beds on the other.

You are not only a guest here. Every stay helps bring the house back to life, and the rooms you sleep in today are part of how the next ones open.

Gudanes is a Class I Historic Monument, which means the walls stay as the centuries left them — bare plaster, weathered stone, protected by law and not ours to change. Everything within them is new: antique French beds dressed in floors relaid by hand, bathrooms finished to a standard the rest of the château has not reached yet. The imperfection is deliberate, and it is the whole argument of this page.
