The Valley Through the Year

Spring
Wildflowers through the parkland, the gardens waking, and the markets filling again after winter. Snow still on the high peaks.
Summer
The pool opens. Long evenings, dinner outside, and the vide-greniers and antique fairs at their best — Mirepoix on a Monday, Foix at the end of July.
Autumn
Virginia creeper turns the gates scarlet. Mushrooms in the woods, the harvest through the valley, and the light that photographers come for.
Winter
Ax 3 Domaines is twenty-five minutes away — eighty kilometres of piste between 1,400 and 2,400 metres. The thermal baths at Ax run 33–38°C all year.

Worth the Drive

Mirepoix

One of the best-preserved medieval bastide towns in France, and the most vibrant market in the Ariège. Monday mornings under the timbered arcades. About an hour.

Grotte de Niaux

Paleolithic paintings on the cave walls, fourteen thousand years old and still vivid. Numbers are limited and must be booked ahead. Twenty minutes.

Château de Foix

A medieval fortress above the town, with a museum tracing the Counts of Foix, crossbow and forging demonstrations, and the valley laid out below. Forty minutes.

Class I protection means the walls stay as they were found. Everything within them — beds, linen, floors, bathrooms — is new, and finished to a standard the building around it has not reached yet. The imperfection is deliberate, and the valley outside is no more polished than the house.

The Valley

What Is Within an Hour

The Ariège holds more caves than anywhere else in the Pyrénées and most of the Cathar castles. Almost none of it is crowded.

14,000 years

Grotte de Niaux

One of the great Palaeolithic sites of Europe, and one of the very few where you walk into the dark to see the original paintings — bison and horses, drawn on the rock by people who lived here. Not a replica.

1244

Montségur

The last Cathar stronghold, on a pog at 1,207 metres. After a siege of months, hundreds were burned below it. The field is still called the Field of the Burned. Forty minutes up on foot, and worth every one.

Kings of Navarre

Château de Foix

A medieval fortress over the town, held by the Counts of Foix — one of whom became Henri IV of France. Ramparts, restored rooms, and the market below on a Friday.

1,800 metres

Plateau de Beille

Sixteen kilometres of climb from Les Cabannes, which the Tour de France has finished on. Sixty kilometres of cross-country pistes in winter; wide open steppe and the GR10 in summer.

1279

Mirepoix

A Cathar town destroyed and rebuilt as a bastide, with a square of overhanging medieval walkways. The market and the brocante are the best in the region.

Three kilometres

Labouiche

An underground river you travel by boat, near Foix. Europe's longest navigable one, and much better than it sounds.

Also within reach: Roquefixade, facing Montségur across the valley and free to walk any day of the year; the Lombrives caves; and Camon, listed among the most beautiful villages in France.
