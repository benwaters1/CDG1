Who bought it, and why

A château is not usually bought by someone from Perth. This one was.

2013

They Had Barely Seen It

Karina Waters bought Château de Gudanes having barely set foot in it. Ninety of the ninety-four rooms could not be entered. The roof had gone, taking five storeys with it in places. Trees were growing inside the building.

It had been empty for most of a century and a half. A developer had planned to divide it into apartments and the permission had been refused, which is the only reason there was anything left to buy.

The First Winter

A Tent, Inside the House

There was no electricity, no heating and no water. She stayed anyway, pitching a tent in one of the rooms to keep warm.

People tell that story back to us more than any other, and it is the one that explains the rest. Nobody does that as an investment. The eight months that followed were spent clearing more than five hundred tonnes of fallen stone, debris and rubble before a single thing could be restored.

Why It Is Open

Guests Are the Reason It Continues

A building of this size cannot be restored privately. Ninety-four rooms, forty-three thousand square feet, and every intervention applied for and approved before a tool is lifted.

The five finished rooms pay for the work on the rest. That is not a fundraising line — it is the actual mechanism, and it is why a night here costs what it does and why the walls stay as they are.
