Bienvenue

Château de Gudanes stands above the valley at Château-Verdun in the Ariège, an hour and three quarters south of Toulouse. Built in 1741, abandoned in 1850, and under restoration since 2013 — a restoration, not a renovation, and one of the most closely followed private projects in France.

She was designed in 1741 by Ange-Jacques Gabriel, principal architect to King Louis XV and the hand behind Marie Antoinette's own Petit Trianon at Versailles. That royal lineage endures today: Gudanes holds the same Class I Historic Monument classification as the Palace of Versailles and the Eiffel Tower.

Abandoned in 1850 and very nearly lost, she has been returned to life since 2013 — room by room, and always by hand. Five hundred tonnes of fallen stone were cleared before the work could begin. Overpaint is lifted patiently to reveal frescoes hidden for generations, and every intervention is approved by France's Monuments Historiques before a tool is lifted. The restoration has been followed by hundreds of thousands, photographed for Vogue, and honoured with the Medal of Honour of the Fondation Prince Louis de Polignac.

Abandoned in 1850. Very nearly lost.

She stood empty for over a century and a half before the roof was made good

Much of her remains as the centuries left her: bare plaster, weathered stone, the quiet marks of two hundred and eighty years. She is protected, and so she is preserved rather than perfected. Within those walls, however, everything within them has been made anew — the floors, the bathrooms, the beds and the linen, and the food that arrives at her table.

Come through the gates and the drive runs a quarter of a mile under the limes before the façade shows itself. Inside, the work is still going on around you — and so is dinner, and the fire, and the morning.

Dine at La Table

A table laid by candlelight, in a dining room brought back from ruin — and cooking that answers to the valley rather than a menu.

The dining room took years to recover; the cooking in it is worth the wait. Mountain cheeses, charcuterie, foie gras, magret de canard, wild mushrooms when the season gives them — bought that morning at the village market, from producers the chef has used for twenty years, and in summer cut from the gardens below the terrace.

Why People Come Back

More than a château, Gudanes is a place shaped by history, connection and the people who pass through her doors.

Whether staying among her ancient walls, gathering around her table, creating something lasting or celebrating life's most precious moments, guests are welcomed into the warmth and spirit of this living estate.

Hers is among the most closely watched private restorations in France, and those who stay here are part of it — not observers of a finished thing, but present while it is still being made.

The château is not finished and does not pretend to be. Guests arrive into the middle of the work, and the work continues because they do.

  • 1741Designed by Ange-Jacques Gabriel, principal architect to Louis XV
  • 94Rooms across 43,000 square feet
  • 1994Classified alongside Versailles and the Eiffel Tower
  • 500tOf rubble cleared before work could begin
  • 2013Restoration begins. It has no end date.

Most châteaux this size are museums, or closed.

Gudanes is neither. Scaffolding stands in rooms that are not ready and dinner is served in one that is. The gardeners are in the beds by seven, the conservators are on the frescoes by eight, and you are having breakfast between the two.

Very few people have slept in a building like this while it was still being repaired. She was bought derelict in 2013, with five hundred tonnes of fallen stone to clear before anything could begin.

Fifteen years on, the conservators still measure a good morning in centimetres of fresco.

Nothing here is funded from outside. The rooms that are finished pay for the ones that are not, which is why the doors opened before the work was done.

Preserving the past. Welcoming the future.

A restoration, not a renovation — the longer course, and the only one worth taking with a house of this consequence. Fifteen years in, and she is not yet finished. We should be glad to welcome you while the work is still unfolding.
