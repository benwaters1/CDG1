Privacy

What we do with your details

Plainly, and without the usual hedging. We are a small house in the Ariège, not an advertising business, and the short version is that we ask for what a stay needs and nothing else.

Last reviewed {{ privacy_reviewed }}. Château de Gudanes, 2 Route de Beille, Château-Verdun, Ariège 09310, France, is the data controller.

What we hold, and why

A room booking
Your name, email, telephone, dates, how many are coming, and anything you tell us in the notes. We need it to hold the room and to write to you about it — that is the contract between us.
A table or an atelier
The same, plus what you cannot eat, and on the longer ateliers anything medical we would need to know. That is health information and we treat it as such: it goes to the chef and to whoever is looking after you, and nowhere else.
An event enquiry
Your name, how to reach you, the dates you have in mind and what you describe. We keep it so we can answer, and so the conversation makes sense if you come back to it months later.
If you stay and you are not a French national
Your surname, first names, nationality, date and place of birth, and your home address. This is the fiche individuelle de police, which French law obliges an établissement d'hébergement to keep and to produce if the authorities ask for it. We collect nothing beyond the fields the law lists — no passport number, and no copy of any document — and it is deleted six months after you leave.
Text messages
If you leave a mobile number when you book, we may text you about that booking and nothing else — where to collect the key, a change on the day, the things you would want to know while you are travelling and away from a computer. You can stop those at any time by telling us, and we keep the number on a do-not-text list afterwards so that nothing puts you back on it. We will never send you an offer or an invitation by text unless you have separately said yes to that; leaving a number on a booking form is not saying yes, and we do not treat it as though it were. We do not text landlines, and a number we cannot read we simply do not use.
The newsletter
Only your email address, and only if you asked for it. Every letter carries an unsubscribe link, and using it stops the letters at once. We keep the address on a do-not-write list afterwards rather than deleting it, because that is the only way to be sure an old list never puts you back on. Ask and we will remove it outright.

We do not buy lists, we do not sell anything to anyone, and we do not build a profile of you.

Paying

Card payments are handled by Stripe. Your card number is typed into Stripe's own form and travels to Stripe, not to us — we never see it and could not store it if we wanted to. What comes back to us is whether the payment succeeded and a reference to look it up by.

Cookies, and the ones we don't set

There is no analytics on this site. No Google Analytics, no advertising pixel, no session recorder, nothing that follows you to another website. That is not a policy position we are proud of so much as a description of the code — you can look at the page source and see it.

Two cookies exist. One remembers your session while you are booking so the pages join up; the other is a security token that stops a third-party site submitting a form as you. Both are strictly necessary and neither tells anyone anything about you.

Who else is involved

A handful of companies do specific jobs for us. They act on our instructions and may not use your details for their own purposes.

Stripe
Takes the payment. Sees your card details; we do not.
Resend
Sends our confirmations and letters, so it handles your email address.
Railway
Hosts the site and the database it reads from.

Three more are simply files your browser fetches while a page loads, which means those companies can see your IP address: Google Fonts for the typefaces, Squarespace for the photographs, and Frankfurter for the exchange rate behind the currency switcher. None of them receives your name or your booking.

Our accounting system holds supplier invoices and staff expenses. Guest bookings are not sent to it.

How long we keep it

Dietary and medical notes
Deleted once the stay is over. There is no reason to keep them and good reason not to.
What you need from the house
If you tell us something about getting around — that you cannot manage many steps, that you use a stick — we keep it against your name rather than against the one booking, so that you do not have to explain it again the next time. It is deleted twelve months after your last stay. You can ask us to forget it sooner and we will.
Booking and payment records
Kept as long as French accounting law requires us to keep them, which is longer than we would otherwise choose.
Enquiries that came to nothing
Deleted twelve months after the date they were about — an event enquiry nobody took up, a waitlist request for dates long past. An enquiry that became a booking is kept as a booking record instead.
Newsletter
Until you unsubscribe. The address then stays on a do-not-write list, so that we cannot accidentally add you again — unless you ask us to delete it.
The police register
Six months after the stay ends, then deleted. French law requires a guest house to keep a fiche for guests who are not French nationals and to produce it if the authorities ask; six months is how long it must be kept, and we do not keep it longer.

The link in your confirmation

Every booking email carries a link that opens your reservation without a password. It works because the link itself contains a long private code, so treat it the way you would treat a password — anyone holding that link can see and change the booking. Forward it only to someone you would be happy to have do that. Tell us and we will issue a new one.

What you can ask us to do

You may ask for a copy of what we hold, ask us to correct it, ask us to delete it, ask us to stop using it, or ask for it in a portable form. Where you gave us consent — the newsletter, the medical notes — you may withdraw it at any time, and doing so costs you nothing else.

Write to ariege@chateaugudanes.com and a person at the château will answer. If we handle it badly you can complain to the CNIL, France's data protection authority, at cnil.fr.
