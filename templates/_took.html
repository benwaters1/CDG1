L'œuvre d'une vie

You have painted a room and known how long it took. This house has ninety-four.

Everyone who has pulled up a floor or lived through a kitchen knows the shape of it — the dust, the delays, the arguments about a doorway, the evening it finally comes right. That feeling is the correct one. Hold it, and then let it run for fifteen years.

None of which is the reason to come.

A house this size was never meant to be lived in by one family, and it does not survive being looked at. It survives being used — fires lit, beds slept in, a long table with people around it. So we have people to stay, and the house goes on.
