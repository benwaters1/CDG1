AvailabilityFive bedrooms, of ninety-four rooms. They open one at a time, as the restoration reaches them.

Five bedrooms inside a restoration followed by Vogue, AD Italia and the French press — a Class I Historic Monument still being brought back, and open to guests while the work goes on.

The walls are bare plaster and weathered stone. They are protected under French law and cannot be changed, so they stay as two hundred and eighty years left them. Everything within them is new and was chosen carefully — the beds, the linen, the floors underfoot, the bathrooms.

Both things are true at once. It is a stay with real limitations: no lift, original staircases, bathrooms shared and privately allocated on the ground floor, and electricity kept deliberately low. These are not oversights — they are the conditions under which a monument of this class is permitted to be lived in at all. In practice it means the evenings are lit by candle, and nobody has ever complained about that.

Each room opens as the last one is finished. That is not a donation — it is how the work continues.

Not a Compromise

Why the Walls Stay As They Are

The bare plaster in these rooms is not unfinished work. It is protected, and putting a modern surface over it would be illegal.

Every intervention here is applied for and approved by the Monuments Historiques before a tool is lifted. Drawings, materials, methods and the people doing it are all reviewed. The process is long and it is strict, and it should be — this is one of fewer than fifteen hundred buildings in France holding the highest grade of protection, and it will outlast everyone currently arguing about it.

What that buys you is a room nobody could build. The plaster you sleep beside was laid in the eighteenth century and has been read, recorded and consolidated rather than replaced. A hotel could give you a smooth wall tomorrow. Nobody can give you this one.

Reversible
New work is done so it could one day be removed without harming what was there
Recorded first
Every surface is photographed and documented before anyone touches it
Lime, not cement
The building has to breathe. Cement traps water and destroys what it covers
Approved, then done
Not the other way round. Unauthorised work on a Class I monument is a criminal offence

Dining

À Table

Candlelight, the fire lit in winter, and whatever the valley gave up that morning.

Continental breakfast every morning, and dinner cooked in the château's own Renaissance kitchen. There is no fixed menu — what appears depends on the Sunday market in the village square, on producers the chef has bought from for twenty years, and in summer on the gardens below the terrace.

Mountain cheeses from the farms above the village, charcuterie, foie gras, magret de canard, wild mushrooms when the season gives them, and desserts drawn from a recipe book written at Gudanes in the 1700s.

Dinner is served by candlelight in the dining room, and in winter the fire is lit. Tell us when you book, and the kitchen buys for it.

What dinner is like

Bathing

Les Salles de Bains

Floral papers, a cast-iron freestanding bath, and a dressing room to yourself.

Three bathrooms on the ground floor, each with its own character.

Émeraude, du Levant and Les Deux Chambres each have a private bathroom a few steps along the corridor. Cerise and Tilleul share a bathroom set between them.

Brass fittings, hexagonal terracotta underfoot, and cast-iron baths deep enough to disappear into. Ensuite bathrooms are not permitted under Class I rules — which is why they sit off the corridor rather than behind your bed, and why they are among the newest rooms in a building that is mostly not.

Things to Do With the Days

Optional experiences to add to your stay — chosen when you book, subject to season and an additional charge.

The Kitchen

A Private Dinner

The dining salon given over to your party alone, cooked by Tom, our chef de cuisine, in the Renaissance kitchen. Menus are built around what the gardens and the Sunday market give that week, and on some days from the château's own eighteenth-century recipe book. Up to forty seated; smaller numbers eat better.

Priced per person on the menu agreed · book at least a week ahead

The Valley Floor

Riding

Horses from a stable in the valley, ridden out along the Ariège and up through the forest tracks above the village. Hacks of an hour or a half-day, with a guide who knows which paths are passable after rain. Suitable for beginners; the mountain routes are not.

By the hour or the half-day · weight and experience asked in advance

Summer

Kayaking the Ariège

The river runs cold and fast off the Pyrénées. Guided descents from twenty minutes above the château, graded gentle to lively depending on the month and the snowmelt. Boats, buoyancy aids and a guide who will read the water for you.

Half-day, May to September · minimum age applies and is set by the river, not by us

Take It With You

A Picnic Basket

Packed by the kitchen the night before: bread from the village, cheese and charcuterie from the Sunday market, something cooked, something sweet, and a bottle if you want one. Basket, cloth and glasses included, to take up the mountain or no further than the parkland.

Per basket, serving two or four · ordered the day before

Late

Dinner by Candlelight in the Salons

The unrestored salons lit by candle alone, after dark, with the plaster and the panelling as they are. No electric light is fitted in those rooms, which is the reason it works. Drinks and a short menu rather than a full dinner.

Weather and wind permitting · not available when work is underway in those rooms

Cellar

A Tasting of Ariège Wine

The Ariège lost almost all its vines to phylloxera and never fully replanted, so what survives is small, local and rarely leaves the department. A tasting of six, poured and explained, with an account of why there is so little of it.

Per person, in the salon or the cellar · arranged with a day's notice

Winter

Skiing

Ax 3 Domaines is twenty-five minutes up the valley — eighty kilometres of piste to 2,400 metres. Skis, boots and helmets brought to the château, and an ESF or ESI instructor for any age and any standard. Plateau de Beille, twenty minutes the other way, is the quieter option for cross-country and snowshoeing.

Equipment and lessons quoted on the day rate · transport arranged

Arrival

Airport Transfer

A private car from Toulouse-Blagnac, an hour and three quarters each way, met at arrivals. Up to three people with luggage. We also meet the train at Foix, which is twenty minutes away and often the easier journey.

€350 each way, Toulouse · Foix quoted separately

Mornings

Yoga on the Terrace

An hour before breakfast on the terrace above the formal gardens, facing the mountain. A local teacher comes up from the valley. Mats provided; the class suits any level, and in poor weather it moves into the music room.

Per session, per group · arranged the evening before

The Valley

Guided Walks

Half a day or a full one, with a guide who has walked these valleys all his life. The high paths above Beille, the abandoned hamlets, and the routes the Cathars took. Lunch is carried up from the château kitchen.

Half day or full day · lunch included

Sunday and Wednesday

Market Mornings

Les Cabannes on a Sunday, three minutes below the gates. Tarascon-sur-Ariège on Wednesday and Saturday, and much bigger. Go with the chef, who has bought from the same producers for twenty years, and bring back what you find for the kitchen to cook.

No charge to go with the chef · buy what you like

Antiques

Les Brocantes

The Ariège is thick with brocantes and vide-greniers, and most of them are not advertised. A guide who knows which dealers merit the detour, what a piece should cost, and how to have anything large shipped home. The fair at Mirepoix is the one to plan around.

Half day or full day · shipping arranged separately

The Estate

Electric Bikes

The valley road runs flat along the river for miles in both directions, and the assistance makes the climbs to the villages above possible for anyone. Helmets and route notes provided.

Per bike, per day
