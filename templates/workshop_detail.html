The Rhythm of Château Life

An Itinerary, Not an Obligation

Fifteen at most, so the days stay unhurried. There is an itinerary, and most people want every hour of it — but nothing on it is compulsory.

Take an afternoon by the pool, a walk up the valley or a book in the music room, and pick the work up again in the morning. Dinner is the one hour nearly everybody keeps.

The house is yours between the working hours: the formal gardens, the parkland running up toward the mountain, the four salons, the tennis court in the trees.

It is a house that is lived in, and it is meant to be used that way.

Guest Quarters

Les Chambres

Each bedroom is prepared with antique French beds, vintage monogrammed linens, heirloom furnishings and objects collected through the restoration. Beyond the windows, the mountains and the valley.

Every bed is an antique French one. Rooms are arranged for two, with a third bed if you need one — say so when you book. Coming alone, you share with another guest travelling solo, or take a room to yourself with a single supplement.

Electricity is limited by design. Rooms are lit by candle in the evening, with power outlets and a bedside lamp.

Bathing

Les Salles de Bains

Brass fittings, floral papers, thick towels and a window onto the valley — somewhere to wash the day off before dinner.

Ensuite bathrooms are not permitted in a building protected like this one, so they sit together on the first floor rather than off each bedroom. On an atelier, as on any stay, each bedroom is given its own.

The Difference

Why Here and Not Elsewhere

The building

A Gabriel château

Designed by the First Architect to Louis XV, who also built the Petit Trianon for Marie Antoinette. The same classification as Versailles.

The work

Real, and permitted

Running these required permission from the Monuments Historiques. What you touch is eighteenth-century fabric, under supervision, and it stays touched.

The table

Voltaire ate here

In the 1750s this was written of as the place to be wined and dined in the Ariège. Voltaire and Diderot made the journey. Dinner is served in the same room.

The village

Forty-one people

Château-Verdun. Les Cabannes, three hundred and seventeen, is three minutes below the gates with the Sunday market and the farm shop.

A centimetre is a good morning.
