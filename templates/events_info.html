The Setting

Rooms in every state between ruin and restoration

Ninety-four rooms across five floors and 43,000 square feet, an eighteenth-century façade by the architect of the Petit Trianon, and parkland beneath the Pyrénées. Some rooms are finished; some are bare plaster and weathered stone. Both photograph extraordinarily.

Four salons, a chapel, two vestibules and the grounds — the main dining room seats forty, the other salons twenty to thirty, and the outdoor areas take larger parties.

What We Can Arrange

The Table

Our own chefs, cooking from the Ariège markets and the château gardens — from a long lunch under the trees to a candlelit dinner in the recovered dining room.

Staying Over

Bedrooms for your closest guests, so the evening need not end at a car park. The rest of the party stays in the valley, minutes away.

The Arrival

Horses and a carriage can be arranged for the drive, which was built for exactly that. Or the bus from Toulouse, which is how most guests come and which is its own kind of arrival.

Gudanes is a Class I Historic Monument, which means the walls stay as the centuries left them — bare plaster, weathered stone, protected by law and not ours to change. Everything within them is new: antique French beds dressed in vintage monogrammed linen, floors relaid by hand, bathrooms finished to a standard the rest of the château has not reached yet. The imperfection is deliberate. A house still being repaired is an unusual place to marry, and that is rather the point.

One Wedding

What Actually Happened

Dan and Carl wrote to ask whether the château would host their wedding. The honest first answer was hesitation — the work was still in progress and it seemed too important a day to hold in a building site.

They persevered, came to see it, stayed the weekend, and it was obvious. Their guests came up by bus from Toulouse through the mountain valleys, not entirely sure where they were being taken.

Daniel, who runs the bistro in the village, gave the guests dinner on the first evening and breakfast every morning after. On the wedding day itself he turned up unannounced with the village choir and held a concert.

It ran four days. Wildflowers, champagne, beer brought over from London, and a forest of cakes. The photographs ran in Vogue Australia.
