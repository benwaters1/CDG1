What You Take

The Whole House, Not a Room In It

One wedding runs at a time. For the days you have it, there are no other guests, no handover halfway through, and nobody else's party in the next room.

Ceremonies happen in the grounds or in the historic interiors. Dinner is in the main dining salon. Above forty, dinner moves outdoors — the terraces and the parkland take larger numbers comfortably.

Be Warned

It Is Not Finished, And That Is The Point

This is a restoration, not a renovation, running since 2013. Under Class I listing the walls stay as the centuries left them — bare plaster, weathered stone, protected by law.

What you and your guests touch is new: the beds, the linen, the floors, the bathrooms. What photographs is old. Couples who want a finished house should look elsewhere, and we would rather say so now than on the day.

How It Usually Runs

Three Days, Not One Afternoon

Almost nobody takes the château for a day. The shape below is what most weekends settle into — not a package, and every part of it moves.

  1. Friday Arrivals through the afternoon. Dinner for whoever is here — usually thirty or so, in the dining salon.
  2. Saturday morning Breakfast long and late. Hair and make-up in the finished rooms; the rest of the party walks or swims.
  3. Saturday afternoon Ceremony in the grounds or the historic interiors. Photographs while the light comes round onto the façade.
  4. Saturday evening Dinner — forty inside, more on the terraces. Then the salons, and nobody has to drive.
  5. Sunday The market at Les Cabannes, then a long lunch. Departures whenever people manage it.

MusicOutdoors until midnight, indoors after. The nearest neighbour is not close, but the valley carries sound further than you would think.

What We Do and What We Do Not

We do

  • The house, exclusively, for the days you have it
  • All the cooking, from our own kitchen
  • Beds for fifteen and breakfast for everyone staying
  • Tables, chairs, linen, glass and the gardens
  • Flowers from the gardens, in season — see below

We do not

  • Plan the wedding. Bring a planner if you want one planned
  • Provide a marquee, a dance floor or a sound system
  • Hold a licence — the legal ceremony happens at the mairie
  • Do hair, make-up, or photography
  • Pretend the building is finished

    Asked Often

    Questions

    Do you hold a wedding licence?

    No. French weddings are legally civil, so couples marry at the mairie — Château-Verdun or Les Cabannes — and the ceremony here is the one everyone watches.

    How many can we seat?

    Forty inside, in the dining salon. More outdoors across the terraces and six acres of parkland.

    Where does everyone sleep?

    Fifteen in the château, in five bedrooms. The village is three minutes down the hill and the valley takes the rest.

    Will there be scaffolding?

    Possibly. We will tell you where before you commit, not on the day.

    Can we bring our own suppliers?

    Yes, for flowers, music, photography and planning. The cooking is ours.

    How far ahead should we book?

    Summer goes a year or more ahead. Spring and autumn are quieter and cost less.
